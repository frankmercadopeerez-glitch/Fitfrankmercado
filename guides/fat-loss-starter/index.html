Free guide · adults 18+

Lose fat without turning your life into a crash diet.

A conservative starting framework for healthy adults: repeatable meals, strength training and activity you can sustain. No promised kilograms and no detoxes.

In this guideWhat mattersYour first weekMeal structureTrack progressSources

Scope: General fitness education, not a diagnosis, therapeutic diet or medical care. Stop exercise for chest pain, fainting or severe unusual shortness of breath and seek appropriate care.

The four things that matter most

  1. Create a modest energy gap through repeatable food choices. NIDDK notes that weight loss requires reducing energy intake, while long-term maintenance depends on habits that can continue.
  2. Keep or build strength. Use full-body resistance training at least twice weekly rather than relying only on cardio.
  3. Accumulate movement. Work gradually toward the U.S. guideline of 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity weekly; smaller amounts still count.
  4. Judge trends, not one weigh-in. Water, sodium, sleep and digestion can move scale weight from day to day.

A practical first week

Day 1 · strength

Full body A — 20 to 35 minutes

Chair squat 2–3 × 8–12 · incline push-up 2–3 × 6–12 · backpack row 2–3 × 8–15 · glute bridge 2–3 × 10–15 · carry or plank 2–3 short sets. Finish each set with about 2–3 good repetitions still possible.

Day 2 · easy movement

Walk 20–30 minutes

Choose a pace where you can speak in sentences. If that is too much, use two 10-minute walks.

Day 3 · strength

Repeat A or use simple substitutions

Split squat to support, floor press with backpack, one-arm row, hip hinge and dead bug. Add repetitions before adding load.

Days 4–7

One more strength day, two walks and one full rest day

Do not compensate for a missed session with punishment. Move the next session and continue.

A meal structure you can repeat

At most meals, build around: a protein food; vegetables and/or fruit; a carbohydrate source you tolerate and enjoy; and a modest source of fat. USDA MyPlate encourages variety across fruits, vegetables, grains, protein foods and dairy or fortified soy alternatives.

  • Breakfast: eggs or yogurt + fruit + oats or whole-grain toast.
  • Lunch: chicken, beans, fish or tofu + rice/potato + a large vegetable portion.
  • Dinner: repeat the same structure; you do not need a special “diet meal”.
  • Snack when useful: fruit with yogurt, milk, nuts or another protein-containing option.

Avoid cutting entire food groups unless medically necessary. Liquid calories and highly snackable foods are often easier to reduce than core meals.

Run this experiment for two weeks

  • Record 2–4 morning body-weight measurements weekly under similar conditions and compare averages.
  • Track completed strength sessions and walking minutes.
  • If the trend is not moving after at least two consistent weeks, change one lever: a smaller portion of calorie-dense food or one additional walk.
  • If hunger, fatigue or training performance deteriorate sharply, the approach is too aggressive.

Evidence used

  • NIDDK: Eating and physical activity to lose or maintain weight
  • Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition
  • USDA MyPlate
  • 2023 systematic review and network meta-analysis of resistance-training prescriptions
  • 2024 umbrella review of interval training and adiposity
